The win-rate drop that never happened

Management saw win rate fall from 82.1% to 60.3% during 2017 and was weighing extra headcount. I analysed 8,800 CRM opportunities to find the cause.

There was no drop. No deal in the data closes before 1 March 2017, so the first "quarter" in the report held a single strong month. Every last month of a quarter wins 78 to 83% of its deals. Every other month wins 49 to 57%. Measured by full quarter, the business is steady at about 61%.

The real opportunity is the mid-quarter gap. Lifting months one and two from 52% to 65% takes overall win rate to 69.8%, worth about USD 1.26M over three quarters, with no new hires.

I started with three hypotheses from an issue tree. Re-testing them on equal periods showed two of my own first conclusions were artifacts of the same reading error, so I dropped them and tested timing instead.

What could make these numbers wrong: only 10 months of closes exist, the USD 1.26M assumes flat closed-deal volume and a USD 2,393 average won deal, and the data cannot say why losses cluster early. The company, Vantage Hardware Co., is fictional and built around a public dataset, so this is a method exercise.

Four hypotheses, tested on equal periods
Capacity Workload and win rate are unrelated across 104 agent-quarters (r = -0.06). Not supported
Speed Inside the same month, fast and slow deals differ by at most 4 points. Not supported
Customer mix Client revenue and headcount are flat. Every sector wins 61 to 65%. Not supported
Timing 49%, 55% and 80% in months 1, 2 and 3 of every quarter, in every region and manager. Supported

1 Structured Business Analysis: Corporate Segment Profit Decline Company sales grew 20.4% while Corporate profit fell 13.6%. I framed the problem before touching any data. Issue trees, SMART, DARCI
The question
How can RevoShion restore Corporate segment profit growth to at least +10% year on year within two quarters, without reversing the segment's sales growth?
What I did
Mapped stakeholders with DARCI, wrote a SMART problem statement, built an issue tree for margin compression and cost-to-serve, and turned each branch into a prioritised hypothesis with the metrics that would prove it.
What I recommended
Set a discount limit with manager approval beyond it, reward more profitable order patterns among the largest accounts, revisit pricing on low-margin bundles, renegotiate shipping, and review segment profit monthly.
Context
Understanding Business Process assignment, RevoU, July 2026. RevoShion is a fictional retailer and no data processing was required.

2 TokoBli Campaign Evaluation: Statistics and A/B Testing The campaign with the most revenue was not the most efficient, and a new product page lifts transaction value 11.3%. Excel, statistics, A/B test
The question
How can TokoBli increase total revenue in its next twin-date campaign, based on how its 2023 campaigns performed?
What I did
Cleaned 10,159 transactions to 10,142, removed 12 revenue outliers with the IQR method, compared the 10/10, 11/11 and 12/12 campaigns by revenue and by revenue per rupiah of discount, then ran a two-sample t-test on the old and new product page.
What I found
  • 11/11 brought the most revenue (Rp 16.62 billion). 12/12 was the most efficient, earning 58.5 per Rp 1 of discount with the smallest budget.
  • Mean transaction value was Rp 830,460 on the new page against Rp 746,103 on the old one, with p below 0.0001.
What I recommended
Roll out the new page before the next campaign, use 12/12 as the efficiency benchmark, and keep Men's Fashion on minimal discount.

3 RevoGrocers Sales Performance: 9 Business Questions in SQL The top categories win on volume, not price. Loyalty is already high everywhere, so the lever is spend per customer. BigQuery SQL, CTEs, window functions
The question
Which categories drive revenue, how do price and buyers relate, and where can the business grow?
What I did
Validated a 7-table grocery database first, then answered nine questions with JOINs, CTEs and window functions. Revenue was calculated the same way throughout: quantity × price × (1 − discount).
What I found
  • Confections leads on volume without being the most expensive per unit.
  • Grain has the highest unit price (61.43) and the lowest volume, a possible price barrier.
  • Repeat purchase rates sit between 93.68% and 99.85% in every category.
What I recommended
Protect stock and promotions for the top four categories, test a Grain bundle, cross-sell to lift spend per customer, and build a reward programme that spreads loyalty beyond a few heavy spenders.

4 RevoBank Customer Segmentation: Cleaning, EDA and RFM in Python 42% of customers drive only 2.4% of revenue, while a fifth of the base drives nearly half of it. Python, pandas, RFM
The question
How can a bank get existing credit-card customers to transact more?
What I did
Cleaned six months of card and user data (5,531 usable cards out of 5,599), measured profit and fraud, then segmented 1,938 customers with RFM. I chose RFM over K-Means because each segment comes with a clear action.
What I found
  • Net profit was Rp 5.64 billion over six months. Fraud was 0.263% of volume yet consumed about 17.6% of gross profit.
  • Growth-ready customers are 20.8% of the base and 44.7% of revenue. The inactive group is 42.1% of customers and 2.4% of revenue.
What I recommended
Upsell the 260 growth-ready clients, win back the 227 at-risk customers one to one, and use a low-cost automated nudge for the inactive group instead of a big re-acquisition push.

5 Revo Hospital Financial Performance Dashboard A Tableau dashboard that located Rp 14.5 billion of pending revenue by department and patient type. Tableau, LOD expressions
Who it is for
The Finance Lead of a hospital network, who opens it monthly to decide where billing follow-up should go. It leads with three KPIs, then narrows down.
What I did
Modelled five related tables with Tableau relationships instead of joins, joined on patient ID because 1,470 of 1,800 patients share a name, and chose each chart for the question it answers.
What I found
  • 14% of net revenue is still pending.
  • Cardiology collects 81.7%, the lowest of eight departments. Self-pay patients collect 64.6% against 87 to 90% for insured patients.
  • Admissions rose from 1,146 in 2024 to 3,854 in 2025, and pending revenue climbed with them.
What I recommended
Review Cardiology billing, require a deposit for self-pay admissions, grow billing capacity with volume, and offer instalments above Rp 20 million. Whether the same patients drive all three problems still needs a combined cross-tab.
